What a church actually needs

Answer the questions in a nervous first-timer's head.

A visitor's anxiety isn't theological — it's practical and social. "Where do I park? What do I wear? What about my kids? Will anyone talk to me?" The site's job is to remove every reason not to come.

They're wondering"When are services, and where do I go?"
The site answersService times and address above the fold — the single most-sought item (43% look for times, 28% for directions).
They're wondering"What is this going to be like — will I stick out?"
The site answersA "Plan Your Visit / What to Expect" page: how long, what to wear, real photos of real people. Unchurched adults want calm and welcome, not hype (Barna: 80% prefer calm).
They're wondering"Is my family safe here — what happens with my kids?"
The site answersClear kids & childcare info — check-in, safety, ages. For families it's a top decider.
They're wondering"Can I get a feel for it before I show up?"
The site answersWatch a recent service online. Online is an on-ramp, not a substitute — only ~5% stay online-only; most who watch also come in person (Pew/Burge).
The message

Help more of the people God is already sending to your door actually walk through it.

To a pastor, this is never about revenue or funnels. It's souls, welcome, and not being the barrier to someone's first step back. Frame the whole conversation around the family checking the site on Saturday night, the newcomer who just moved to town, the dechurched neighbor quietly ready to try again.
